Research

My interests sit where plant molecular biology meets breeding: how crops sense heat and pathogens at the molecular level, and how those findings, including through genome editing, can make wheat production more stable.

NRF project · 2025 – present

A heat-responsive PUB E3 ligase and the transition to flowering

As part of a National Research Foundation of Korea project on reproductive transition and spikelet development for early-maturing wheat, I lead the functional characterization of a heat-responsive plant U-box (PUB) E3 ubiquitin ligase. The work spans plant transformation, expression analysis, recombinant protein expression with MBP, His and GST fusions, protein interaction assays and heat-stress phenotyping of overexpression lines in Arabidopsis. I am drafting the manuscript.

  • Transformation
  • Protein interaction
  • Thermotolerance
RDA project · 2025 – present

Wheat response to Fusarium head blight

In a Rural Development Administration project on shortening maturity and reducing Fusarium head blight damage, I inoculate wheat with Fusarium asiaticum spores, compare resistance- and susceptibility-associated gene groups, and quantify their expression by qRT-PCR.

  • Spore inoculation
  • qRT-PCR
  • Plant–pathogen interaction
RDA projects · 2025 – present

Heat and genetic resources in the field

I support two field programs: setting damage criteria for abnormal temperatures across growth stages of winter cereals, where I help run high-temperature treatments, and seed genetic resource conservation, where I help cultivate accessions and record traits. In the lab, I am planning a heat time-course in wheat seedlings to follow when stress-responsive genes switch on and off.

  • Field trials
  • Trait characterization
  • Time-course design

Looking ahead

I want to connect the genes that matter in the field to the mechanisms that explain them.

For my PhD I am looking for labs in wheat genetics and plant pathology where I can move between finding candidate genes and proving their function, through genotype–phenotype analysis in populations or through transgenic and genome-edited validation.

I am especially drawn to how crops interact with heat and pathogens, and I am keen to build stronger quantitative and computational skills on top of my molecular background.
